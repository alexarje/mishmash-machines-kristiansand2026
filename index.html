Rent or run

The first fork in the road. Rent a commercial service, or run open models on hardware you control. And a middle road most people forget.
Rent. The best models today, zero setup, any device. But no weights, no reproducibility, data leave the room, terms change, and it is the same model for everyone. Click: run it yourself. Full control, private, trainable on your own material. One step behind the frontier, you carry the setup and the energy bill. Click: the middle road. Open models on somebody else's GPUs: Hugging Face, Mistral's own platform, EU-hosted providers, or an institution-hosted chat service such as UiO GPT. Open weights, rented hardware. Bottom line: six questions to ask of any tool.

Six questions to ask of any tool

Which model?

Name and version. "AI" is not an answer.

Where does it run?

Your machine, your institution, an EU data centre, the US.

Where do my data go?

Prompt, files, recordings. Stored? Trained on?

Who owns the output?

And can you sell a work made with it?

Same result next year?

Reproducibility for research; continuity for a repertoire piece.

What does it cost?

Per month, per hour of GPU, per kilowatt-hour.

Depth slide. The six questions apply to every option in the rest of the map. For a researcher the fifth is reproducibility. For a musician it is whether the piece can be performed again in five years.

Your own hardware

The layer artists, teachers and students can most realistically own. Seven rungs from a microcontroller to a workstation.
Left to right: Arduino and ESP32 for keyword spotting and gesture classes, milliseconds, no language model. Raspberry Pi and Jetson for small vision and audio models, an embodied agent in a box. Phone and tablet: on-device models of one to three billion parameters, camera and microphone, always with you, closed platforms. Laptop: seven to fourteen billion chat, Whisper, RAVE on stage. Laptop with a discrete GPU: diffusion, fine-tuning small models. Workstation: seventy billion locally, training small models, serving a studio. Click: memory. Click: which models fit where. The one number that matters is memory: it decides which model fits at all. Compute only decides how fast.
Depth. Rule of thumb for language models: parameters times bytes per parameter. A quantised 7 or 8B model is about five gigabytes and runs on any recent laptop. Thirty billion needs about twenty. Seventy needs forty: a machine with a lot of unified memory or two big GPUs. Full precision seventy billion is a server job. Real-time audio models are tiny by comparison.

What each rung is good for

Microcontroller

  • keyword spotting, gesture classes
  • sensor fusion, milliseconds
  • no LLM, ever
  • wearables, instruments

Pi / Jetson

  • small vision and audio models
  • Whisper tiny, YOLO
  • an embodied agent in a box
  • installations that run for a year

Phone / tablet

  • 1–3B on-device LLMs
  • camera, mic, sensors
  • closed platforms, app stores
  • the device every pupil has

Laptop

  • 7–14B chat, offline
  • Whisper transcription
  • RAVE and nn~ on stage
  • the studio in a bag

Laptop with GPU

  • Stable Diffusion, FLUX
  • fine-tune small models (LoRA)
  • real-time everything
  • hot, loud, one hour of battery

Workstation

  • 70B local, video generation
  • train RAVE overnight
  • serve a whole studio or class
  • needs a room and a power bill

School tablet or laptop

  • managed device, no GPU
  • Feide login, pupils' data
  • everything must be hosted
  • the most constrained rung

Rule of thumb

  • memory decides what fits
  • compute decides how fast
  • prototype on the smallest rung that works
Depth. Rung by rung. Note the school tablet or laptop: managed, locked down, no GPU, so everything has to be hosted somewhere else. That is the WP4 problem in one card.

Shared compute

When your own hardware is not enough. Five steps up a stair, each with more compute and more paperwork. Removed from the slide: institutional · national · European.
Shared server: a GPU box in the lab, ssh, first come first served. VDI: a virtual desktop for licensed software and sensitive data, rarely a real GPU. Institutional HPC: UiO's Fox, NTNU's IDUN, UiA's DGX servers at CAIR, local clusters at UiB and UiT. Slurm, batch jobs, free for the institution's own people, students through a supervisor, collaborators added by a project leader. Most other institutions have their own small servers or nothing. National: Sigma2 and NRIS. Olivia, 448 Grace Hopper chips after the February expansion, plus Saga; application rounds twice a year. KI-fabrikken, opened last year, is the first national channel that explicitly includes business and public sector. European: LUMI in Finland, where Norway is a member, and the EuroHPC AI Factories. Click: what changes as you go up. More compute, more people can share it, data can live next to it. But more waiting, more paperwork, less interactivity. Batch scheduling is the wrong shape for a rehearsal.

How you actually get on

Institutional clusters

  • UiO Fox: Educloud project, PI adds collaborators
  • NTNU IDUN: supervisor requests access
  • UiA: DGX servers at CAIR
  • UiB and UiT: local HPC via the IT division

NRIS / Olivia

  • project application, CPU and GPU hours
  • two rounds a year
  • any Norwegian research institution
  • data storage next to compute

KI-fabrikken

  • Sigma2, part of the LUMI AI Factory network
  • research, business, public sector
  • expertise and support, not only GPUs
  • artists and cultural institutions: unclear

LUMI / EuroHPC

  • through NRIS or EuroHPC calls
  • AMD GPUs: check your software
  • large allocations, long lead times

NAIC (paused)

  • the low-threshold national GPU service
  • funding ended 31 March 2026
  • "no-cost hibernation" since April
  • successor being procured by Sigma2

Sensitive data

  • TSD at UiO, SAFE at UiB, HUNT Cloud at NTNU
  • GPU capacity inside is limited
  • health and pupils' data live here or offline
Depth. The practical routes. Note NAIC: it was the one national service designed for low-threshold GPU access, and it has been paused since April while Sigma2 procures a permanent successor. That is a live hole. Sensitive data: WP3 and WP4 material cannot go to Olivia; it lives in TSD-type environments or fully offline.

Frontends

Hardware and models are only half. The tool you sit in decides who can use it at all. Removed from the slide: how you talk to the model.
Four families. Chat: the web apps, an institution-hosted service like UiO GPT, GPT NTNU or Copilot under UiB's Microsoft agreement, or Open WebUI and LM Studio on top of a local model; this is where teachers and most artists start. Code and agents: VS Code with Copilot or Continue, Claude Code, Cursor, Jupyter, Educloud On Demand on Fox. Creative tools: Max with nn~ and FluCoMa, Pure Data, Ableton with Neutone, TouchDesigner, ComfyUI for image and video, Unity and Unreal, p5.js with ml5. Programmatic: Python with PyTorch, transformers and diffusers, llama.cpp, MLX, Ollama or vLLM as a local server. The same model can sit behind all four.
The trick that makes switching cheap: almost everything speaks the same OpenAI-compatible API. Ollama and LM Studio on the laptop, vLLM on a lab server or behind a tunnel to Fox, Mistral and the EU providers, and the commercial APIs. Change one line, keep the workflow. Prototype against the laptop, point at a server for the show.

Tool is not model

Depth. The distinction people most often miss. Top row: tools, the thing you sit in. Click: the models underneath. ChatGPT and UiO GPT are both GPT. VS Code can talk to GPT, Claude or a local Llama. Max with nn~ runs RAVE, and can call a language model too. ComfyUI drives FLUX or Stable Diffusion. Ollama serves Llama or Mistral. Same model in many tools, same tool with many models. When someone says "we use AI", ask which model, where it runs, and where the data go.

Conditions

Community licences and terms of use

  • Llama: "Built with Llama", 700M-user cap, derivatives named Llama
  • Gemma: prohibited-use policy passed on
  • Stability community licence (SD3.5, Stable Audio Open): free under $1M revenue
  • CreativeML OpenRAIL-M (SD 1.5, SDXL): use restrictions travel with the weights
  • NorwAI NorLLM: Nordic organisations

Non-commercial

Research only, CC-BY-NC

  • FLUX.1 dev: non-commercial licence for the model
  • MusicGen: code MIT, weights CC-BY-NC 4.0
  • many research checkpoints on Hugging Face
  • fine for a paper; a commissioned work is commercial
Depth. Three colours with concrete names. Green: permissive, do what you like, keep the notice. Amber: community licences and terms of use; usually fine for artists and researchers, but with strings: attribution, revenue caps, naming rules, use restrictions that travel with the weights. Red: research-only or non-commercial; fine for a paper, a problem for a commissioned work. Two traps: the code and the weights can have different licences, as with MusicGen, and licences change between versions, so read the card of the exact checkpoint. Licences: RAVE MIT; MusicGen weights CC-BY-NC; Stable Audio Open and SD3.5 Stability community licence with a one-million-dollar revenue cap; FLUX.1 schnell Apache 2.0 but FLUX.1 dev non-commercial; Llama community licence; Gemma terms of use; Qwen3 Apache 2.0. Rule of thumb, not legal advice.

Where to find them, how to trust them

Find

  • Hugging Face: weights, model cards, datasets
  • Ollama and LM Studio libraries: one-click local
  • IRCAM Forum: RAVE and nn~
  • the paper's GitHub, for the newest

Trust

  • model card: training data disclosed?
  • evaluation: on what, by whom?
  • reproducible: pinned version, checksum
  • energy: training and inference cost stated?

Who needs what

Now the people. MishMash has seven work packages, and they do not need the same machine. Removed from the slide: seven work packages, seven different answers.
Two axes: how much compute, and how sensitive the data. Click. WP1, a musician on stage: milliseconds, a laptop in the room, own recordings; bottom left. WP2, a filmmaker: video generation, hours on a GPU, and the rights of the training data are the issue. WP3, a music therapist: small models but health data, so TSD or fully offline; top left. WP4, a teacher: no GPU at all, a managed tablet or laptop, pupils' data, and a need for free Norwegian tools; far left, high up. WP5, cultural institutions and rights organisations: production-grade, contracts, audit trails. WP6, the National Library: a multimodal model on the whole collection, petabytes, Olivia or LUMI for months; far right. WP7, designers and rescue operators: physics and law built in, often on an embedded device. And the computer science researcher who builds the models for all of them: a lot of compute, mostly open or synthetic data, bottom right. No single machine serves this picture. That is why there is no common solution.
Depth. The musician. Record, train overnight, play in Max. The model is thirty megabytes, trained on your own sound, runs without internet.
Depth. The filmmaker. The open route is a step behind Runway and Sora, and the rights are yours to defend. Compare with the Julius production tonight at Kraftverk.
Depth. The teacher. Not a compute problem, an access and language problem. The scarcest resource on the whole map is a free, Norwegian, GDPR-safe creative AI tool for a classroom.
Depth. The National Library. Petabytes, months of allocation, and then an open release that everyone downstream can use. The loop closes.

Observations to argue with

  • The academic infrastructure is built for batch science, not for a rehearsal room.
  • The people who most need cheap, controllable, rights-clean models have the least access.
  • Access depends on which institution you sit in, not on what you need.
  • Open Norwegian models exist for language and speech, and for nothing creative.
  • Nobody has defined "artist", "teacher" or "cultural institution" as a user of national compute.
  • The low-threshold national service (NAIC) is paused; its successor is not yet here.
Depth. Six observations, each one an invitation to disagree.
